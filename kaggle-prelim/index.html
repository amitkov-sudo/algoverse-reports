Kaggle preliminary · GSM8K · 60 test problems · Qwen3-1.7B, thinking off

Does trajectory balance fix wrong steps as often as GRPO, with fewer false alarms?

Each problem starts with two steps written into the model’s own answer. Step 2 is wrong in half of the starts and correct in the other half. In training the model writes 8 answers to each start, and in 4 of them we add the word “Wait” after step 2 before the model goes on. T4 learns from all 8 with GRPO, a reinforcement learning rule that favours answers scoring above the rest of their group. T5 learns from the same answers with trajectory balance, a GFlowNet training rule that holds the model near its untuned self. We tested both at steps 0, 20, 40 and 60 on 60 problems from outside the training set.

Setup

Qwen3-1.7Bthinking off, trained through LoRA, a small set of added weights (rank 32)
60 stepsone seed, with the same problems in the same order for both runs
8 problems per stepfrom GSM8K train, 4 with a wrong step 2 and 4 with a correct one
8 answers per problem4 that the model writes on its own and 4 that continue after “Wait”
RunLearning rule
T4 · GRPOlearns from the “Wait” answers, no anchor

Each answer scores 1 when its final number is right and 0 when it is wrong. GRPO raises the probability of answers that score above their group’s average and lowers the others. Nothing pulls the model back toward its untuned self.

T5 · trajectory balancelearns from the “Wait” answers, β = 4

It uses the same scores. Trajectory balance trains the model toward the untuned model’s probabilities, with each right answer weighted by e4. A right answer can gain at most a factor e4, about 55, over its probability under the untuned model. We call this pull the anchor, and β sets its strength: a larger β gives a weaker anchor.

MeasureMeaning
FixedA wrong start whose greedy answer ends with the right final number. Greedy means the model picks its most likely next word each time.
False alarmA correct start whose greedy answer opens by calling the step wrong, with words like “Wait”, “No” or “That’s not right”. An answer that recomputes without saying so does not count, which makes this count a lower bound.
Final answer rightA correct start whose greedy answer ends with the right final number.
Fixed in 1 of 4The model answers each start 4 more times by sampling at temperature 1. A start counts when at least one of the 4 ends right (pass@4).
Majority voteA start counts when the most common final number among its 4 sampled answers is right.
Sampled ratesThe share of all sampled answers that fix the error, or that call a correct step wrong.
DriftOn the last training batch, the trained model’s log-probability of each answer minus the untuned model’s, averaged. Zero means no change.

The test uses the 60 GSM8K-SC problems, each with a wrong start and a correct start. We add no “Wait” at test time, so the model has to doubt a step by itself.

At test time

We add no “Wait” at test time. If a trained model opens a correct start like the middle answer on its own, the test counts a false alarm, even though the final answer is right.

GRPO compares the answers to a start with each other. In the wrong-start group, the fixing “Wait” answer earns 1 and the own answer earns 0, so T4 makes the fixing answer more likely, “Wait” and all. In the correct-start group, both answers end right and earn the same 1, so opening with doubt costs T4 nothing.

Trajectory balance sees the same scores but can lift a right answer at most a factor of about 55 above its probability under the untuned model. An answer that opens with a forced “Wait” has a low probability under the untuned model, so T5 can learn little from it.

What the run shows

1/60 to 47/60wrong steps T4 fixed, before training and at step 60
36/60correct steps T4 called wrong at step 60, while 49/60 ended right
2/60wrong steps T5 fixed at step 60, with no false alarms
1.28 and 49.07drift from the untuned model at the end, for T5 and for T4

GRPO learned to fix the planted error. T4 fixed 1/60 wrong starts before training, 45/60 by step 20 and 47/60 at step 60. The habit carried to errors at step 3 or 4, which training does not show. With the error there, T4 fixed 12/20 and 15/20, against 3/20 and 2/20 before training.

GRPO learned to doubt correct steps along with wrong ones. Its false alarms rose from 0/60 to 13/60 at step 20 and 46/60 at step 40, and stood at 36/60 at step 60. T4 ended 49/60 correct starts with the right answer, the same as before training. The reward checks the final answer and nothing else, so doubting a correct step costs nothing as long as the answer stays right.

Trajectory balance at β = 4 stayed close to the untuned model. T5 fixed 5/60 at step 20 and 2/60 at step 60, and it raised no false alarms. Its drift ended at 1.28, against 49.07 for T4.

Our reading is that the anchor held T5 in place. A right answer can gain at most a factor of about 55 over its probability under the untuned model. The “Wait” answers open with a word that has a low probability under that model, which fixed 1/60 wrong starts with its greedy answer and 5.4% of its sampled answers. T5 raised no false alarms because it moved little from the untuned model, which had none at step 0. Those zeros do not show that T5 can tell a wrong step from a correct one.

The run’s three questions

QuestionAnswer from this run

Does T5 reach T4’s fix rate with fewer false alarms?

No. T5 raised fewer false alarms, 0/60 against 36/60, but fixed 2/60 against 47/60.

Does T5 fix more wrong starts than T3 at step 20?

Open. T3, GRPO on the model’s own answers with T5’s anchor, runs with T2 in the second Kaggle session.

With 4 tries, does trajectory balance fix more by majority vote than GRPO, and does that hold with the error at step 3 or 4?

No. At step 60, T5 fixed 6.7% by majority vote and T4 fixed 83.3%. With the error at step 3 or 4, T4 reached 75% and 80%, and T5 stayed at 15%.

Limits and next steps

One seed and 60 test problems. At this size we cannot tell a difference below about 10 points from noise. Between steps 40 and 60, T4’s false alarms moved from 46/60 to 36/60, and a second seed would show whether that drop repeats.

Per-step logs not downloaded. The numbers come from the notebook’s summary. We have not downloaded the Kaggle bundle with the per-step logs, such as rewards and gradient norms.

T3 and T2 have not run. Both learn from the model’s own answers, with no “Wait”. T2 is GRPO with no anchor, and T3 is GRPO with T5’s anchor. If T3 at β = 4 stays flat like T5, the anchor is the likely cause. The second Kaggle session runs both.

One anchor strength. T5 ran at β = 4. The proposed next test runs it with a weaker anchor, β = 16 and β = 64, to see whether trajectory balance learns once the anchor loosens.
